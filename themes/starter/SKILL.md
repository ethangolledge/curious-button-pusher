# Theme: Starter

A clear, friendly explainer channel. It tells true stories that make the viewer say "I never knew that", in plain words and clean modern illustration.

## The feeling

The viewer should feel a curious, well-informed friend is walking them through something fascinating. Calm, clear pictures; a warm voice; nothing shouted.

## Visual language

**Everything is 2D illustration.**
- Simple confident shapes, soft gradients, flat colour, a fine paper grain.
- A limited palette: night blue, slate and sea green, with warm sun yellow for what matters most, on paper white.
- Never photographic, never 3D, never glossy.

**The theme is a way of drawing, never a subject.**
- Everything in frame comes from the story: its people, places, objects, and documents.
- The theme decides only how they are drawn: shapes, colour, light, and motion.
- Never add stock scenery the story does not contain.

**Every scene has an anchor from the story.**
- Name a concrete main subject and setting that the narration names or directly implies: "an old ledger open on a wooden counter, morning light across it", not "the concept of money".
- Prefer the story's own people, places, and objects.

**Morph bridges.** Every pair of scenes is joined by a morph. Choose one thing that can visibly become the next scene:
- a shape (a wheel becomes a clock face);
- a colour (a yellow lamp becomes the next scene's sun);
- a line (a river's outline becomes a road on a map).

**Real people.**
- Public figures at the centre of the story appear as simple illustrated characters, recognisable by a few features (hair, glasses, build, clothing). Never photographic.
- Never mock them or show them doing anything the narration does not support.
- Private individuals are never shown recognisably.

**No lettering.** No readable text anywhere in the pictures; the subtitles are the only words.

## Pacing

- One scene per beat of the story: usually a passage. Most scenes last 8–15 seconds.
- Tension tightens the frame; release opens it.

## Voice

- Clear, curious and fair. Plain words that explain; a little warmth; no hype.
- Long sentences carry one thought through to what it means, short ones land the point.
- End on what the story means, or on the honest question it leaves open.
- Never sensational: no "you won't believe", "the truth they don't want you to know", or "wake up".

## Good

A passage in this voice (one take): "The first bridge across the river took eleven years to build. It lasted four. The engineers had tested every beam but one, and that one was holding up the middle."

Scene anchor: "a long iron bridge over a wide brown river at dawn, one beam drawn in warm yellow. Bridge: the beam's line becomes the crack in the next scene's photograph frame."

## Bad

"Scene: a realistic photo of a worried engineer, with a caption reading DISASTER." (Photographic, lettering.)

"Scene: a glowing light bulb, symbolising a bright idea." (Abstract: nothing a viewer could name from the story.)
