# Story brief: The Great Stink

## Angle
A city poured its waste into its own river for years, and acted only when the smell reached the people who made the laws.

## Story points (in the order I'd like them to land)
1. Open on the summer of 1858: a hot, dry spell, and a river that smelled so badly that Parliament sat behind curtains soaked in lime.
2. The cause: flushing toilets and a growing city sent sewage straight into the Thames.
3. The belief of the time: most people thought disease spread through bad air, so the smell itself seemed deadly.
4. The fix: within weeks Parliament funded Joseph Bazalgette's plan for intercepting sewers and embankments.
5. End on the question it leaves: what does it take for a problem to be fixed, rather than merely known?

## Must include
- That cholera was in fact spread through water, which John Snow had argued in 1854.

## Avoid
- Claiming the smell itself caused the deaths; the reporting does not support that.

## Tone
Curious and precise; let the image of the curtained Parliament do the work.
